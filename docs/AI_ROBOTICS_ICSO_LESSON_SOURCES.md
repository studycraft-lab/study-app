# Grade 6 ICSO: Artificial Intelligence and Robotics

The interactive lesson is at `/study/icso/ai-robotics`. It has six short missions (23 estimated minutes) and six original final questions (about 6 minutes). The model and robot pathway are diagrams, not a trained AI or a controlled robot.
The result stays on the lesson page and is not yet saved to the StudyCraft progress dashboard.

## Sources and scope

- Saved SOF synopsis: `ingestion-artifacts/icso-grade6-synopses/09-artificial-intelligence-and-robotics.html`. The lesson covers AI tasks, speech recognition, NLP, computer vision, machine learning, robots, expert rules, predictive search and the synopsis's human-intelligence vocabulary. The saved HTML repeats the chapter text.
- Recent downloaded papers, newest first: `ICSO - 2025_Class_6_2296_answer.pdf` asks about NLP, humanoid-robot claims and a human-intelligence term; `ICSO - 2024_Class_6_7530_answer.pdf` asks a three-statement AI item and an odd-one-out human-intelligence item. The app's final questions use original examples and statement pairs.
- The downloaded mock series adds robot examples and real-world-use questions. The lesson teaches the concepts behind those examples rather than requiring brand-name robot recall.
- The 2025 paper uses **intrapersonal** intelligence, absent from the saved synopsis's five human-strength examples. The lesson defines it beside **interpersonal** as a small paper-informed extension. These labels describe human strengths in the paper, not AI domains.
- The signed-in SOF chapter question bank was not available for this build. Review it before claiming complete exam coverage.

## Accuracy audit

| Concept | Cross-check |
| --- | --- |
| AI makes predictions or decisions toward human-defined goals; outputs can be wrong | [NIST AI glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/artificial_intelligence), [IBM AI overview](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/artificial-intelligence) |
| NLP works with human language; computer vision works with images | [IBM NLP](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/natural-language-processing), [IBM AI types](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/artificial-intelligence-types) |
| Machine-learning recommenders use patterns in data to suggest items | [IBM recommendation engines](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/recommendation-engine) |
| A robot can execute a fixed program without AI | [NASA on automation and autonomy](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/nesc/verification-and-validation-challenges-for-autonomous-gnc-technology-for-nasas-next-generation-missions/) |
| The human-intelligence labels come from the multiple-intelligences framework, not AI-domain taxonomy | [Harvard Project Zero](https://pz.harvard.edu/projects/multiple-intelligences?qt-social=1) |

The synopsis says AI machines “think and learn like humans” and calls AI the “brain” of robots. The lesson uses more precise wording: AI systems perform specific recognition or prediction tasks, and robots may use fixed instructions or AI. The 30-minute lesson is a core introduction; it does not cover every robot type, use case or human-intelligence label from the synopsis.
