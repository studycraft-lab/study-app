# Founder validation plan: 16 weeks, INR 2 lakh

## Decision this plan must produce

At the end of 16 weeks, decide whether to continue a bootstrap-first service for **ICSE Grades 5-7 Science and Social Studies**, based on real paid and repeated behavior. The target is a working beta and approximately 25 families who each make a genuine payment after no more than one free reviewed chapter.

The plan does not attempt to validate all boards, Grades 3-10, tutoring agents, handwriting, voice, stylus input, school sales, or publisher partnerships. Grade 3 can be used as a usability stress test in the founder's household but should not be counted as proof for the commercial wedge.

## Offer and scope lock

**Positioning:** “Turn this exact chapter into reviewed written-exam practice by tomorrow. See which points your child can and cannot answer.”

**Free sample:** one adult-reviewed chapter for a qualified family with a real upcoming test or revision need.

**Paid offer:** after one free reviewed chapter, charge **INR 99 for the next three chapters or 30 total pages**, one child, valid 30 days. Then test a second purchase at **INR 199 for five chapters** or **INR 299 for ten chapters/100 pages**. Record gross price, discount, net receipt, refund, relationship to founder, and whether the buyer was directly asked by the founder. The INR 99 payment tests checkout and repeat use; only the second purchase begins to test sustainable willingness to pay.

**MVP modalities:** parent-uploaded page images/PDFs; child types answers on phone, tablet, or computer. Do not add voice, photographed handwriting, stylus, open tutoring, or English Literature to rescue poor activation. Those create different hypotheses.

**Concierge promise:** reviewed bank within 24 hours on working days. Manual onboarding and adult review are allowed, but every minute is logged.

## Success is a conjunction, not one vanity number

### Commercial outcome

- **Target:** at least 25 distinct families pay INR 99, producing INR 2,475 before refunds, and at least ten subsequently buy the INR 199 or INR 299 pack.
- At least 15 payers must be beyond the founder's closest family/friend circle.
- No more than five payers may be “support payments” where the child never completes a session.
- At least 40% of activated free-chapter families pay INR 99, and at least 40% of mature INR 99 users make the second purchase, or the cohort reaches 25 first payments and ten second payments from no more than 60 qualified parent leads.
- Refund/credit rate at or below 10%.

### Activation and child behavior

- At least 70% of families receiving a reviewed bank have the child finish the first ten-question session within 48 hours.
- At least 50% of activated children complete three sessions within 14 days.
- Median ten-question completion is at least 70%; abandonment and reported fatigue are segmented by grade and question type.
- At least half of sessions after onboarding happen without the founder/reviewer prompting the child directly.

### Repeat value

- At least 60% of paying families submit a second distinct chapter within 30 days or before the child's next school test, whichever is later within the study window.
- At least 50% of payers active by Week 8 return in a later assessment cycle by Week 16.
- At least 60% of measured parents save 30 or more minutes of active preparation/checking time per chapter, supported by a short before/after time diary rather than recall alone.

### Trust and quality

- Fewer than 2% of reviewed questions contain an objective content, source, answer-key, or playability defect.
- Fewer than 5% of subjective grades are overturned after an informed adult reviews the source, rubric, child answer, and model feedback.
- Fewer than 10% of subjective attempts require low-confidence escalation after calibration.
- At least 80% of parents can explain why a representative subjective answer received its score after seeing rubric points and citations.
- No unresolved high-severity privacy, security, or rights incident.

### Operational economics

- Reviewed bank delivered within 24 hours for at least 80% of uploads.
- Median adult QC converges to five minutes or less per new chapter by Week 12; 90th percentile below ten minutes.
- Median support time is at or below 20 minutes per family per month by Week 12.
- Cash CAC is at or below INR 700 in at least one repeatable non-founder channel; record founder time separately.
- At least 30% of payers come from unpaid referral/community propagation by Week 16.

These thresholds are intentionally demanding. Twenty-five favorable interviews, 25 free users, or 25 payments obtained through personal obligation do not meet the decision standard.

## Kill and pause criteria

Stop or materially pivot the wedge if any of these occur after the specified learning opportunity:

1. **Payment failure:** fewer than 10 of 40 activated free-chapter families pay INR 99, the project cannot reach 25 first payments from 60 qualified leads, or fewer than 25% of eligible trial users make the second INR 199-299 purchase.
2. **Activation failure:** fewer than 40% of families that receive a reviewed bank complete one session, after two clearly documented onboarding iterations.
3. **Repeat failure:** fewer than 30% of payers use a second distinct chapter when they have another test/revision event.
4. **Trust failure:** reviewed-question defects remain above 5%, or more than 10% of subjective grades are overturned, after two calibration rounds.
5. **Operations failure:** median adult QC remains above ten minutes per chapter or support above 45 minutes per family per month by Week 12.
6. **Acquisition failure:** cash CAC remains above INR 1,500 and fewer than 20% of payers arrive through referral/community after the first cohort.
7. **Child-value failure:** fewer than half of activated children finish three sessions, or most use requires continuous parent/founder prompting that offsets the time-saving promise.
8. **Rights/privacy blocker:** qualified counsel finds the proposed private-upload commercial flow requires unavailable licences or material controls that cannot fit the budget/timeline.
9. **Safety blocker:** a material child-data isolation, consent, or processor problem cannot be fixed before further onboarding.

A near miss may justify a four-week extension only if one clearly isolated variable can be tested without broadening the product. Do not continue because the founder has already spent the sabbatical.

## Cohort and recruitment design

### Definition of a qualified lead

A parent is qualified when all are true:

- child is in an English-medium school in Grades 5-7 for the core cohort;
- family uses ICSE for the beachhead cohort;
- parent or child has a Science or Social Studies chapter relevant to a test/revision event in the next 30 days;
- parent currently spends time checking learning or pays for supplementary help; and
- parent can lawfully provide the pages and gives required consent.

Maintain two labelled exploratory cells, not pooled into wedge metrics:

- 5-8 CBSE families to compare source standardization and competition;
- the founder's Grade 3 household plus at most three external Grades 3-4 families for usability only.

### Relationship coding

Code every family:

- R0: founder household;
- R1: immediate family/close friend;
- R2: colleague/friend-of-friend;
- R3: community/referral with no prior relationship;
- R4: paid cold acquisition.

Report conversion and retention by code. R0/R1 evidence is useful for usability but weak for willingness to pay and trust transfer.

## Weekly execution

### Weeks 1-2 — Problem behavior and baseline

**Goals:** establish how families actually prepare; recruit outside the immediate circle; create the measurement system.

- Interview the existing five parents and five students separately using the interview kit.
- Recruit at least 15 additional parents and 12 students, including at least eight R2/R3 families.
- Ask each parent to reconstruct the most recent test and show artifacts: chapter, school notes, worksheet, guidebook, messages, or timetable.
- Collect a seven-day parent preparation-time diary from at least ten families.
- Observe two full parent-child revision sessions with consent; do not rely only on opinions.
- Publish two simple landing-message variants: exam outcome versus parent time saving. The call to action is “submit a chapter for the reviewed beta,” not “join an AI waitlist.”
- Instrument a single funnel ledger with source, grade, board, subject, relationship, test date, upload, bank-ready, first session, third session, payment, second chapter, support, QC, refund, and referral.
- Engage counsel with a diagram of the actual data/content flow, processor list, notices, retention options, and screenshots.

**Gate at end of Week 2:** at least eight of 20 interviewed parents show recent behavior consistent with active questioning/checking, and at least five supply a real upcoming chapter. If pain is only hypothetical, stop before building commercial plumbing.

### Weeks 3-4 — Five-family concierge alpha

**Goals:** validate end-to-end delivery and learn where manual work hides.

- Onboard five families one at a time, at least two outside R1.
- Help the parent upload; time every step and note every clarification.
- Produce the bank with the existing ingestion workflow; perform adult review with a fixed checklist.
- Return within 24 hours and observe the child's first session live or through a consented screen recording.
- Have the parent check five random questions and three subjective grades against the source.
- Run a post-use interview before showing pricing.
- Ask for the full INR 99 Founding Trial payment only after the child has used the free bank. Record a completed payment, not verbal intent.

**Gate:** four of five banks delivered, three children activate, no severe source/privacy issue, and at least two deposits. Otherwise fix the single largest funnel break and repeat with five more before proceeding.

### Weeks 5-6 — One-free-chapter funnel

**Goals:** test activation outside high-touch founder relationships and calibrate quality.

- Enrol 15-20 additional qualified families from R2/R3 channels.
- Standardize upload instructions and a 15-minute optional onboarding slot.
- Randomize, where volume permits, a “24-hour reviewed” versus “instant draft then reviewed” experience. Do not expose an unsafe unreviewed bank without a clear draft label.
- Blind-sample at least 20% of questions for a second adult check.
- Collect 100 or more authentic subjective answers for the grading benchmark.
- Reveal the INR 99 three-chapter offer only after the free chapter; use one price in checkout.
- Test INR 199/five chapters versus INR 299/ten chapters as the real second purchase; defer annual pricing until repeated chapter-pack use exists.

**Gate:** 70% first-session activation, at least half complete three sessions, reviewed defect rate below 5%, and at least five full beta payments or conversions from deposits.

### Weeks 7-8 — First paid cohort

**Goals:** prove use after payment and expose support burden.

- Grow to 8-10 paying families.
- Require no more than one free chapter per household.
- Start second-chapter follow-up based on the family's real test calendar, not a generic reminder cadence.
- Compare typed subjective completion by grade, device, answer length, and parent presence.
- Run an adult/model blinded grading audit; adjudicate disagreements and update rubrics/prompting, not just the model score.
- Create a weekly unit-economics snapshot from real tokens, pages, QC minutes, support, payment fees, and acquisition.

**Midpoint decision:** continue only if at least five families have paid, three have a second chapter or a scheduled near-term event, and no core metric is already past its kill threshold.

### Weeks 9-10 — Channel cells

**Goals:** find at least one path beyond founder asks.

Run small, separately tagged experiments:

1. **Parent referral:** one personal referral invitation after a successful second session; no spam/contact upload.
2. **WhatsApp/community:** an admin/member posts the offer with a sample and test-event call to action.
3. **Colleague/LinkedIn:** founder story focused on written-exam preparation, with a real chapter CTA.
4. **Facebook/Meta:** two message cells with small equal budgets; optimize to qualified upload, not clicks.
5. **Parent webinar/demo:** one 25-minute session showing source-to-rubric workflow and answering privacy questions.

For each, calculate impressions if available, qualified leads, uploads, activation, payment, second chapter, cash spend, founder time, and reviewer time. Stop cells producing cheap but unqualified leads.

**Gate:** at least one R3/R4 channel produces three or more payers with cash CAC below INR 1,000 and activation no worse than 15 percentage points below warm cohorts.

### Weeks 11-12 — Operational convergence

**Goals:** reach 15-20 payers and prove the founder can eventually step back.

- Give a trained reviewer the documented QA checklist; measure without founder intervention.
- Let new families self-serve the standard upload before offering help; compare completion and errors.
- Set confidence thresholds for subjective grades and route only uncertain cases to review.
- Test default source deletion after extraction/QC versus consented time-limited retention only if counsel approves and families understand the tradeoff.
- Review every support issue and eliminate the top two recurring causes through copy/process changes.
- Interview non-payers and one-session churned families; do not offer further free service before learning why.

**Gate:** median QC at or below five minutes, support at or below 20 minutes/family/month, and at least half of mature payers have a second chapter.

### Weeks 13-14 — Repeat exam cycle and final acquisition

**Goals:** reach approximately 25 payers without degrading quality and observe return behavior.

- Re-engage only around known test/revision dates, with parent-controlled reminders.
- Compare parent-reported preparation time with baseline diaries.
- Ask payers for a referral only after a completed second chapter or clear success event.
- Spend the remaining channel tranche only on the best measured channel.
- Conduct two tutor discovery interviews and one publisher discovery conversation if warm access emerges; do not redirect the beta into B2B.

### Weeks 15-16 — Decision, not polish

- Freeze new experiments early enough to observe activation.
- Re-interview at least eight payers, five non-payers, and five churned/low-use children separately.
- Audit metrics by relationship, grade, board, subject, device, channel, and reviewer.
- Recalculate unit economics with actual usage and time.
- Record legal/control actions completed and remaining.
- Classify the outcome using the decision table below.
- Write a one-page continuation plan capped at 10-15 founder hours/week, or a shutdown/data-deletion plan.

## Experiment ledger

Use one row per family and immutable event timestamps. Required fields:

- anonymous family ID; relationship code; acquisition source and cash spend;
- board, grade, subject, publisher/edition, pages, file type, device;
- consent and assent versions; retention choice;
- upload start/complete; help minutes; OCR/generation start/complete;
- model/token/retry costs; reviewer minutes; defects by type;
- bank-ready time; question count and coverage status;
- first/third session; completion; parent presence; prompts/reminders;
- objective defects, subjective appeals, adult adjudication;
- offer shown, price, coupon, paid date, net receipt, refund;
- second chapter and later exam-cycle event;
- support minutes and reason; referral request and result; and
- baseline/after parent preparation minutes.

Use coded evidence in notes: **OBSERVED**, **ARTIFACT**, **SELF-REPORT**, **PAYMENT**, **BEHAVIOR**, **INFERENCE**. A positive quotation is never stronger than the behavior attached to it.

## Metric definitions

- **Qualified lead:** meets the five qualification conditions above.
- **Upload completion:** sufficient, legible, ordered pages plus title/edition metadata received.
- **Bank delivered:** reviewed, playable bank available to the correct family.
- **Activated family:** child completes at least one ten-question session.
- **Three-session activation:** child completes three sessions within 14 days of bank delivery.
- **Payer:** non-founder household transfers non-trivial money and does not receive it back; track R0/R1 separately.
- **Second-chapter retention:** a different chapter is uploaded and used, not regeneration of the free chapter.
- **Question defect:** wrong source claim, wrong answer/rubric, missing required context, duplicate that damages coverage, citation failure, or unplayable item.
- **Subjective overturn:** informed adult adjudication changes awarded rubric points materially.
- **Cash CAC:** attributable cash channel spend divided by new payers from that channel. Report founder/reviewer acquisition time separately.
- **Preparation time saved:** baseline active parent minutes minus comparable product-assisted active minutes; waiting time is not parent labor.

## Decision table at Week 16

| Outcome | Evidence shape | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Pursue | 25+ genuine payers; repeat, quality, operations, legal, and CAC thresholds largely met | Continue bootstrap; automate operations; keep wedge fixed for another cohort |
| Pursue conditionally | 15-24 payers with strong activation/repeat and one isolated solvable acquisition or pricing issue | Four-week bounded extension; no new modality/board |
| Pivot | Strong parent pain and payment, but child self-use or upload workflow fails consistently | Test tutor-operated or parent-only assessment service with a new explicit plan |
| Stop | Weak payment/repeat, unsafe grading, unworkable rights posture, or persistently negative service economics | Stop onboarding, honor deletion/refunds, preserve only consented aggregate learnings |

## Post-sabbatical operating constraint

Before continuing, produce a weekly operating budget for 10-15 founder hours. At 25-100 families, the founder may personally handle escalations, but routine chapter review and support need a trained playbook. Do not raise capital to avoid answering retention and cost questions. Preserve the option to raise only after repeated paid cohorts, a credible rights/privacy posture, and evidence that capital accelerates a working distribution engine rather than subsidizes concierge loss.
