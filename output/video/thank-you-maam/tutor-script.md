## Source and teaching guardrails

- The textbook is an adaptation. Use its version as the source, rather than filling gaps from other editions of the story.
- Roger is described as looking fourteen or fifteen, not given a confirmed age. Mrs Luella Bates Washington Jones is the woman whose purse he tries to steal.
- The strap breaks, but Roger falls instead of escaping. Mrs Jones makes him pick up the purse, asks if he is ashamed, then takes him home. She first holds him firmly; later she lets him go to the sink.
- The door is open when Roger first washes his face. Later Mrs Jones leaves her purse on the bed while she cooks behind a screen. Roger chooses a place where she can see him because he does not want to be mistrusted.
- Roger wanted blue suede shoes. Mrs Jones suggests he could have asked; she remembers having wanted things she could not get, and says she has done things she will not tell him. Do not invent what she did.
- She makes lima beans, meat and cocoa. She does not question Roger about his family or home in ways that might embarrass him; she talks about her work in a hotel beauty shop.
- At the end she gives him ten dollars to buy the shoes, warns him against snatching purses, and says goodnight. Roger manages to say “Thank you, Ma’am,” though he wants to say more. The story says he never sees her again; it does not show what he buys or does later.
- Key word forms selected for this lesson are **snatch**, **tug**, **frail**, **willow-wild**, **supper**, **suede**, **presentable**, **mistrusted**, **embarrass**, **devilish**, and **barely**. Definitions in this lesson are explanations, not quotations from a glossary.

## Scene 1 — A purse at eleven

**VISUAL:** A nighttime sidewalk, a large shoulder purse, Roger approaching, then a simple fall silhouette. Avoid making the attempt look clever or glamorous.

**SAY:** Welcome to *Thank You, Ma’am*, adapted from a story by Langston Hughes. At about eleven o’clock at night, a boy tries to snatch a woman’s purse. *Snatch* means to grab suddenly. The strap breaks with one tug, a sharp pull. But the purse is heavy. Its weight and the boy’s weight make him lose his balance, and he falls on his back instead of running away. The woman catches him. Notice that the story begins with a wrong choice, but that is only the beginning of what we learn about the boy.

**ASK:** Why does the boy fail to escape with the purse?

**REVEAL:** The strap breaks, but the combined weight of the boy and the purse throws him off balance. He falls on the sidewalk.

## Scene 2 — Mrs Jones and Roger

**VISUAL:** Mrs Jones holding the purse and speaking firmly to Roger, then the two walking towards her home. Keep the street safe and uncluttered.

**SAY:** The woman makes the boy pick up her purse and asks whether he is ashamed. He apologises in a whisper. He looks about fourteen or fifteen, frail and willow-wild. *Frail* means slight or weak-looking; *willow-wild* suggests a thin, restless appearance. She asks whether anyone at home tells him to wash his face. He says no. She takes him to her home, saying she will wash his face and teach him right from wrong. Her name is Mrs Luella Bates Washington Jones. The boy’s name is Roger.

**ASK:** What does Mrs Jones do after catching Roger, and what two names should you remember?

**REVEAL:** She makes him return the purse and takes him home to wash his face. The woman is Mrs Luella Bates Washington Jones; the boy is Roger.

## Scene 3 — The open door

**VISUAL:** A small kitchenette with the door open, a sink, warm water and a clean towel. Roger glances between the door and Mrs Jones.

**SAY:** Inside her home, Mrs Jones turns Roger loose at the sink. She tells him to let the water run warm and gives him a clean towel. The door is open. Roger could dash away down the hall, but he does not. He washes his face. When he asks whether she will take him to jail, she says she needs to cook supper and notices that he may be hungry. *Supper* means an evening meal. This scene matters because Roger now has a real choice: run from her, or stay and see what happens.

**ASK:** What opportunity does Roger have at the open door, and what does he choose?

**REVEAL:** He has the opportunity to run away. Instead, he stays and washes his face at Mrs Jones’s sink.

## Scene 4 — Blue suede shoes

**VISUAL:** Roger’s imagined pair of blue shoes appears as a small inset beside the real kitchen. Keep the imagined item clearly separate from the story’s events.

**SAY:** Mrs Jones guesses that Roger may have been hungry enough to try stealing. He explains that he wanted a pair of blue suede shoes. *Suede* is soft leather with a brushed surface. The shoes are what he wanted, but they do not make stealing right. Mrs Jones tells him he could have asked her. He is surprised. The chapter invites us to hold two facts together: Roger made a harmful choice, and he is also a young person with a wish and a need for attention.

**ASK:** What did Roger want, and how does Mrs Jones say he could have tried to get it?

**REVEAL:** He wanted blue suede shoes. Mrs Jones says he could have asked her instead of trying to snatch her purse.

## Scene 5 — She remembers being young

**VISUAL:** Mrs Jones seated on a day-bed, speaking; Roger listening. A small thought bubble holds unlabelled wishes, never invented scenes from her past.

**SAY:** Mrs Jones tells Roger that she was young once and wanted things she could not get. She says she has done things she would not tell him. She does not describe those things, and the story does not invite us to invent them. She is not pretending that wrongdoing is harmless. She recognises that people can make mistakes and still be treated as people. She asks Roger to sit while she makes food and suggests that he comb his hair so he will look presentable. *Presentable* means neat enough to meet others.

**ASK:** What does Mrs Jones reveal about her younger self, and what does she leave unsaid?

**REVEAL:** She says she too wanted things she could not get and has done things she will not tell Roger. The chapter does not specify what those actions were.

## Scene 6 — Trust and the unattended purse

**VISUAL:** Mrs Jones cooking behind a screen; her purse remains on the day-bed. Roger sits on the far side of the room, within her view.

**SAY:** Mrs Jones goes behind a screen to cook. She does not watch Roger or the purse she has left on the bed. Roger could take it, yet he moves to the far side of the room so that she could see him if she wished. The text tells us why: he does not want to be mistrusted now. *Mistrusted* means thought to be dishonest or unreliable. The purse remains within reach, but Roger begins to act differently. Mrs Jones’s trust gives him room to show that change.

**ASK:** Why does Roger sit where Mrs Jones can easily see him?

**REVEAL:** He does not want her to mistrust him. Choosing to remain visible helps him show that he will not take the unattended purse.

## Scene 7 — Food without prying

**VISUAL:** A table with lima beans, meat and cocoa. Mrs Jones and Roger eat; a small hotel beauty-shop sign appears as she talks about her work.

**SAY:** Mrs Jones heats lima beans and meat, makes cocoa, and sets the table. As they eat, she does not press Roger about his home or family—questions that might embarrass him. *Embarrass* means make someone feel awkward or ashamed. Instead, she talks about her work in a hotel beauty shop that stays open late. The meal is more than food. Roger receives ordinary care and conversation after a moment when he expected punishment. His choice to stay and her choice not to pry both matter.

**ASK:** What does Mrs Jones serve, and what topic does she avoid during the meal?

**REVEAL:** She serves lima beans, meat and cocoa. She avoids questions about Roger’s home and family that might embarrass him.

## Scene 8 — Ten dollars and a warning

**VISUAL:** Mrs Jones offers a ten-dollar note. An inset of the blue shoes remains an idea; never show Roger buying them.

**SAY:** After the meal, Mrs Jones gives Roger ten dollars for the blue suede shoes. She also warns him not to snatch her purse, or anyone else’s. She calls such stealing a devilish act—something seriously wrong that can bring painful consequences. Her gift does not excuse his attempt to steal. It shows that she believes he can choose a better way. The story never shows Roger spending the money, so remember only what the chapter actually tells us.

**ASK:** What two things does Mrs Jones give Roger before he leaves?

**REVEAL:** She gives him ten dollars to buy the shoes and a clear warning not to steal purses again.

## Scene 9 — More than “thank you”

**VISUAL:** Mrs Jones opens the front door. Roger stands in the warm doorway, trying to speak. Then the door closes and the street remains quiet.

**SAY:** At the door, Mrs Jones wishes Roger goodnight. Roger wants to say something more than “Thank you, Ma’am,” but he cannot find the words. He barely manages that short thank-you before she shuts the door. *Barely* means only just. The chapter ends by saying he never sees her again. We may infer that the kindness affected him deeply, because he struggles to express what he feels. But we do not know what happens to him later. His silence gives the ending its force.

**ASK:** What does Roger manage to say, and what does the story leave unknown?

**REVEAL:** He manages to say “Thank you, Ma’am.” The story does not tell us what he wanted to add or what he did after he left.

## Scene 10 — Words and choices to remember

**VISUAL:** Five-step path: attempt → firm response → open door → trust and meal → gift and goodbye. Word cards show exact forms from the chapter.

**SAY:** Tell the story back in five steps. Roger tries to snatch a purse and falls. Mrs Jones responds firmly and takes him home. An open door gives him the chance to run, but he stays. She trusts him, feeds him, and avoids questions that might embarrass him. Finally, she gives him money for the shoes and tells him not to steal again. Now remember the language: *snatch*, grab suddenly; *tug*, sharp pull; *suede*, soft brushed leather; *presentable*, neat-looking; *mistrusted*, doubted; *barely*, only just. Use those exact chapter words in your own retelling.

**ASK:** Which detail best shows Roger beginning to change: the open door, the unattended purse, or the ten dollars? Explain your choice.

**REVEAL:** The open door and unattended purse both show his choice to stay and earn trust before he receives the money. An answer should explain what he does, not only name an object.

**AFTER:** You have finished the revision. Pause here and retell the whole chapter aloud, from the broken purse strap to Roger’s difficult goodbye. For an exam answer, support an idea about Mrs Jones or Roger with a precise action from the story.
