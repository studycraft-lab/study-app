## Short tutorial: How to form a question tag

A **question tag** is a short question added to a statement: *She can swim, can't she?* It usually checks or confirms what the speaker thinks is true.

### Card 1. Reverse the positive or negative meaning

| Statement | Tag | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Positive | Negative | *They are ready, aren't they?* |
| Negative | Positive | *They aren't ready, are they?* |

### Card 2. Keep the helping verb and replace the subject with a pronoun

Use the statement's helping verb (*is, have, can, will,* etc.) in the tag. Change a name or noun to a pronoun: *Arjun can swim, can't he?* If the statement has no helping verb, use **do, does, or did**: *Riya sings, doesn't she?*; *They left, didn't they?*

### Card 3. Watch for words that are negative in meaning

*Nobody, no one, nothing, never, rarely, seldom,* and *hardly* make the statement negative in meaning, so the tag is positive: *She rarely complains, does she?* Use **they** for *everyone, someone,* and *nobody* when the person is unspecified; use **it** for *nothing*.

### Card 4. Remember the special patterns

- *I am late, aren't I?* but *I am not late, am I?*
- *Let's begin, shall we?*
- *There is a problem, isn't there?*
- For a command or request, **will you?** is a standard choice: *Please close the door, will you?* Other tags can express a different tone.

**Three-step check:** Is the statement positive or negative? Which verb belongs in the tag? Which pronoun refers to its subject? Put a comma before the tag and a question mark at the end.

**Worked example:** *Nobody has finished the puzzle, ______?* **Nobody** makes the statement negative in meaning, so the tag is positive. **Nobody** becomes **they**, and **has** changes to **have** to agree with **they**. Answer: *have they?*
